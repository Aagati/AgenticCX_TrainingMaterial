# Post-Lunch · H3 — Team Exercise: Capstone Scoping

**Track:** Team Exercise | **Time box:** 40–50 min | **Ships:** a one-page capstone brief
**Pattern practiced:** scoping a real CX problem to one journey, one channel, gate-able metrics, and an eval plan

## Objective
As a team, choose and scope **ONE** real CX problem you will build for the Week-4 capstone, and complete the one-page brief template: Problem, Channels, Success Metrics, and Eval Plan — using everything from today as your toolkit.

## Steps
1. As a team, brainstorm 3–5 candidate CX problems (one per industry vertical is a good starting spread) — then pick **ONE**. Prefer a single channel and a single customer journey over something broad.
2. Open `Capstone_Brief_Template.md` and read the worked example in the Appendix for the level of specificity expected.
3. Write your Problem statement: who is the customer, what are they trying to do, and why does it currently need a human?
4. Define target Success Metrics: resolution rate, CSAT and cost-per-contact — as numbers, not ranges (e.g. "≥75% resolution", not "improve resolution").
5. Write your Eval Plan: how many golden conversations will you need, what will your LLM-as-judge rubric check, and what are your eval-gate thresholds (reuse the gate logic from Lab H2 this afternoon)?
6. Present your one-page brief to another team in 2 minutes. Ask them: is this specific enough that you could start building tomorrow?

## What "ships" means
A completed one-page `Capstone_Brief_Template.md`, specific enough to hand to a facilitator as your Week-4 starting point.

## Files
- `Capstone_Brief_Template.md` — the fill-in template. Complete the four numbered sections; leave the Appendix worked example as reference only.

## Facilitator tips
- The single biggest failure mode at this stage is scope: teams default to "automate all of customer support." Push hard for one journey, one channel.
- If a team's metrics are still vague after Step 4 ("improve CSAT"), don't let them move on — a metric that isn't gate-able isn't finished.

## Discussion (bring back to the group)
If you only had budget to build ONE of the three eval-gate checks from this afternoon before your capstone demo, which would you pick, and what risk are you accepting by skipping the other two?
